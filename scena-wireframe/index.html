02 · From Restaurant Context to Product Focus

Ring was already at the restaurant

My teammate runs a sushi restaurant, and I sometimes help out there. Ring cameras were already in place, and their alerts said when a moment of activity occurred.

I know a box was set down, but will it still be in that spot later?

An alert can answer the first half. The second half is about the state of the space over time, and connecting those moments was still the owner's job.

03 · Shaping the Core Experience

What the owner asks, and what the system does

What should this space look like?Choose a reference and Care Spots.Saves the baseline and regions.
What looks different?Run a check and review the photos.Validates the image, compares selected areas, presents an observation.
Does this need my attention?Choose a follow-up.Keeps the observation and the decision separate.

The owner defines what to watch.

Scena does not assume what normal looks like. The owner picks the expected state, then marks the areas that matter.

  • 1Expected state. The selected photo becomes the reference, with its capture time above it.
  • 2Owner-defined region. The Care Spot outline is drawn by the owner. It is not an AI detection.
  • 3Named by the owner. The owner can name each spot, up to three per camera.

04 · Designing for Uncertainty

The system observes. The owner decides.

A visible difference is not an operational follow-up. A box near the exit might be a delivery or a problem, and only the owner knows which.

  • 1Photo evidence. Before and After, each with its own time.
  • 2System observation. AI describes what looks different, in words such as “Looks like something new”.
  • 3Owner judgment. Needs review, No action needed or Not actually a change.

Couldn't compare ≠ No change

A dark, blocked or stale image is not evidence that nothing changed. Looks similar means a comparison happened. Couldn't compare means it didn't, so it gets its own result and its own actions.

Stand-in: the Looks different crop is cut from the hero's Compare, because no choice is selected there. Its sentence differs from the screen above.

Owner choiceWhen it couldn't compare
Needs review✓ Allowed
No action needed✓ Allowed

Not actually a change corrects a false positive. A comparison that never happened has nothing to correct.

History keeps both

The observation and the owner's decision stay on separate lines. A later decision leaves the original observation in place.

05 · From Prototype to Working Product

I used my interactive prototype to keep the implementation aligned with the MVP

I built this front-end prototype with Claude Code and Codex to communicate the intended flows, interactions, and UI. It served as an implementation reference while Sungwoo built the final application and Ring integration.

PrototypeReviewing the scope

When the initial build included features beyond the MVP, I reviewed the scope against my prototype.

ImplementationCapture time, reflected in the UI

Retrieving an image does not mean it was just captured. So each photo shows its capture time, and a stale image is not presented as a fresh check.

Ring events apply to the whole camera, not to one Care Spot. So the link reads Review related events, and the events are clues, not an exact time or cause.

QAChecking the build, then refining the experience with Sungwoo

Walking through the build, I checked interaction logic, UI consistency, and alignment with Ring's visual language, including colors, icons, and interface patterns. I documented discrepancies in Figma and Markdown, along with the expected behavior and what to prioritize. From there, we refined the experience before submission.
